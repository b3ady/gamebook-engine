# THE NIGHT SHIFT — Theme bible (approved at gate 1, 2026-11-02)

Hollow Hour #2, by Corvin Wake (Grave Choice Gamebooks). 200 sections, 7 endings. Live doc: https://claude.ai/artifact/5LXcfAC9FjPETqNwn3zWGw

## Pitch
01:40 at St Bede's General (a fictional English district hospital). Mum has been in theatre for three hours. You have been her carer for six years. Your older sister Ros flew in from Toronto this afternoon, and the surgeon wants to know which of you makes decisions. Then the lights go to night setting and a nurse in a 1970s starched cap comes to do her round. She is very kind. She would like to look after you too. For ever, if you like.
Hook: *The hospital never lets anyone go. Tonight, neither will you.*

## People
- **You**: mid-thirties (born 1991), still in your home town, Mum's carer for six years since her first stroke. Anger, not guilt: you are the one who stayed. Your care is love and also a place to hide. Reader picks DAUGHTER / SON / KID. Mum calls you **Bean** ("the size of a broad bean on the first scan"); Ros used to, then stopped.
- **Ros (Rosalind)**: older sister, 39, structural engineer in Toronto for eight years. Sends money and careful emails. Jet-lagged, over-organised, takes notes on her phone (each note feels like an audit). Fixes things; carries surgical scissors in her bag (can cut a wristband field off at key moments). Must be rescued from her own composure.
- **What broke**: at Dad's funeral eight years ago Ros announced the Toronto job. You said: *Off you go, then. Someone has to actually be here.* Since then: birthdays, logistics, *fine*.
- **Mum (Maureen, "Mo")**, 71, retired school dinner lady, filthy laugh, lottery syndicate. Emergency repair of a burst aneurysm, one-in-three odds. Trained as a nursing auxiliary at St Bede's in 1979. **Mum lives** in the true ending.

## The secret (two private requests)
- Eight years ago Mum told Ros: *Go. Take the job. If you stay, your sister will let you do everything and never have a life.* Ros was sent, and let you think she left.
- Six years ago, after the first stroke, Mum told you: *Stay with me. Don't let them put me anywhere.* You were asked, and let Ros think you chose.
- Three years ago Mum signed a health Lasting Power of Attorney and advance decision naming **Ros**. Her letter: *Because you would never let me go, love, and I couldn't make you be the one.*
- The reader can learn these in either order, or never.

## The hospital's lie
It offers Mum, **Kept**: not dying, not recovering, on the Old Wing where the night never ends and someone always takes her obs. You only have to sign the consent form as next of kin.

## The place
1960s concrete district hospital bolted onto a Victorian fever hospital. Hospital-at-night made wrong: vending machines a note too low, floor lines (yellow to X-ray, green to Outpatients) leading somewhere new, WRVS tea bar shutter half up, hand-gel dispensers clicking as you pass. It is always the night shift; car-park lights in fog. The **Kept** from every decade (winceyette 1952, a boy in 1980s football kit, a woman with a Walkman), smiling, never discharged. Corridors longer going than coming back. Your name keeps getting written down.

**NIGHT STAFF NOTICE, St Bede's, 1979** (the Handbook equivalent; laminated, in a linen cupboard):
1. Visiting hours are over. Do not tell anyone you are a visitor.
2. If the Night Sister calls you by a patient's name, do not answer.
3. Never lie down on a ward bed.
4. After 3 a.m. the lifts go down only.
5. Ward 0 is closed.
6. Do not sign anything.
7. If the lights dim to night setting, keep walking.
8. (torn off — found later as THE LAST RULE: *Let them go.*)

## The entity: the Night Sister
Care with nothing left in it but routine. Starched cap, silver buckle, upside-down fob watch, red-filtered torch. Warmest voice in the building; never hurries; rounds every hour, each closer to your bed. She doesn't hunt, she **admits**: a line at a time until you're a patient. Kindness is the weapon (tea, blanket, a bed for an hour, a call to theatre) and every comfort accepted is written down. Staff: porters pushing empty trolleys looking for someone to fill them; theatre staff in paper masks with nothing behind; the Kept (harmless unless you lie down beside them). Late reveal: her face is assembled from every nurse who was kind to you, Mum's included. She cannot: follow you through a door you were told never to open; read a name spoken by someone who loves you; keep anyone who has been let go (finale turns on this).

## Signature mechanic: the WRISTBAND (replaces Claim)
Five fields on the Adventure Sheet: NAME, DATE OF BIRTH, NEXT OF KIN, WARD, BED. Filled when you accept her care, give details, die, or are found on a round.
- 4 fields: true ending closed ("the recovery room won't let a patient visit a patient").
- 5 fields: turn to **Admitted** (Final Call equivalent).
- Fields come off only when claimed back: someone who loves you says the true thing; Ros can cut a field off with her scissors at set moments.
**ROUNDS (clock, replaces Hours)**: six boxes 01:00–06:00; tick on each return to the Main Corridor from a zone (first arrival from Act 1 doesn't tick). ≤4 at the finale = reward (Mum's lucid moment); 5 = one more field; 6th tick = Admitted.
Nerve, Vigour, Fortune, Fear (limit Nerve+3), checkpoints, codewords, items, Story/Survival modes as in The Layover. Property bags hold Mum's things (ring, glasses, lottery ticket); returning them matters in endings.
Relationship codewords: **SPOKE** (first honest exchange), **HELD** (you protect her unasked), **MENDED** (real reconciliation). Ros taken by the hospital: **WARDED**.

## Structure
- **Act 1 (~38)**: family room on Level 3 at 01:40; Ros arrives; the surgeon's question; the first round; lights to night setting; the Notice in the linen cupboard; the first fair, warned death.
- **Hub: the Main Corridor (~12)**: lifts, tea bar, chapel door, floor lines, Lost Property (a Larkvale Airways luggage tag — Layover cameo). Hub scenes: Ros, the phone, the Sister's offer.
- **The Old Wing (~30)**: 1890s fever wing, the Kept, the chapel, Sister's office. Item: **VISITOR'S BADGE** (proves you aren't a patient).
- **The Basement (~30)**: Medical Records (Mum's file, the LPA), Property (her ring), mortuary. Item: **MUM'S LETTER**.
- **Maternity & Ward 9 (~30)**: where you were born (1991); the children's ward where Mum slept in a chair by your bed for a week when you were eight. Item: **THE LAST RULE**.
- **Act 3: Ward 0 & Recovery (~45)**: the 1979 recovery ward where Mum did her first night shift; every bay Kept; Mum in the last bay, smiling, obs perfect and unchanging; the Sister with a consent form missing only your signature. Anomaly loop = **the obs chart**: walk the ward, read Mum's chart; nothing wrong → go on; something wrong (a reading in Ros's handwriting, a time that hasn't happened, your name in the patient box) → turn back. True climax: Ros reads Mum's name aloud and you hold her hand; the Sister's hold breaks.

## Voice and differences from The Layover
Second person, present tense, British. Someone who has learned to stay calm on hospital phone lines: precise, wry, worn. Short sentences when the round comes. Ros talks in lists. Mum is funny and rude. Kindness is the threat (being *looked after*). A working-class NHS world and a real 1979; the Sister's kindness was learned from people like Mum. New set pieces: the lift that only goes down; the porter's empty trolley that follows you; a ward of the Kept doing their obs in unison; Property returning Mum's ring warm; the mortuary fridge door that opens on a 1991 incubator; a nurses'-station phone ringing with Mum's voice from tomorrow.
