01:40. The family room on Level 3.

It is called a family room because of the box of tissues. Two settees in wipe-clean blue, a print of a lighthouse, a rack of leaflets about what happens next. In the corner the vending machine hums a note too low, the hum of something switched on in 1985 that expects to outlast you.

You have been her carer for six years, since the first stroke. You know her drug chart better than your own phone number. You know which voices on the out-of-hours line will put you through to a doctor. You have learned to stay calm on the phone. You are staying calm now.
