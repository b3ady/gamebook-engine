# THE NIGHT SHIFT, Round 1, Reviewer A (craft and continuity)

## 1. Verdict: 7.5/10

The book is much better than a first draft has any right to be. The voice holds: precise, wry, worn. Kindness-as-threat is delivered in set pieces (the tea list, the cup marked 14/3/91, the Sister reading your notes back, E_KEPT, E_ONLYCHILD). Mum is funny and rude on the page even when she's absent. The Ward 0 chart loop is a good use of the "Ros's handwriting at three o'clock" beat. What holds it at 7.5: (a) the Ros arc is resolved cheaply and early, and the same confession is hard-coded four times with different furniture, and three of the four are false for SON/KID readers; (b) anger, the bible's engine, is mechanically punished (A34 costs Fear, A35 apology earns SPOKE), so the book drifts into guilt; (c) a cluster of reader-conditional falsehoods and small continuity breaks; (d) tics (Ros "doesn't say anything", hand "stayed there", "unhurried", "six years", the made-up trolley tableau) that thin the best material. Fix 1-4 below and it is an 8.5.

## Ranked problems

1. **Anger is punished, apology rewarded (§189 A33, §63 A34, §114 A35, §150 A06).** Ros quotes your funeral line; "'I meant it,' you say" gets Gain 1 Fear and "Good to know where we are"; "'I shouldn't have said it'" gets SPOKE, a warm lift and Ros standing next to you. The bible says anger, not guilt. A06 ("I do. I'm her carer") also costs Fear. Fix: A34 earns SPOKE too (honest, not kind) and Ros answers with one line of her own anger ("Then say the rest"), so both are real conversations. Make A35 the cheap one: Ros softens, nothing is said, and she keeps her list. Drop the Fear on A06.

2. **Mum's request, §8 M22, §169 W21, §47 R41: the line is false for SON/KID.** All three print "your sister will let you do everything"; only §174 B09 has the three-way variant the canon demands ("your brother" / "our kid"). Fix: use the B09 triple in all four. Add it to any test where Ros quotes Mum.

3. **Same scene, four different sets (§174 B09, §169 W21, §8 M22, §47 R41).** The Legion kitchen "sink with her hat on" (B09) becomes "the night after... plates still out" (W21), "ham still out under the cling film" (M22), "the sandwiches" (R41). The stroke-unit grip is "her good hand round your wrist" (B09, W21), "had my hand" (M22), "got hold of my sleeve... end of her bed... couldn't do her buttons" (R41). Readers who see two will think someone is lying. Fix: lock one version (Legion kitchen, sink, hat on, offer letter; second night on the stroke unit, good hand round the wrist) and reuse its details; vary only Ros's framing line.

4. **The Ros arc is cheap and front-loaded (§120 A10, §3 H04, §26 W19, §114 A35; §169 W21 / §174 B09).** SPOKE comes from four trivial asks; HELD from seven acts, including walking her (§187 W04) and the three rescues (§81 W31, §104 R15, §188 B22); MENDED is available inside the first Old Wing visit (W19→W21: light a candle, tell her why you stayed). By about a third in, the Ros arc is done and she becomes a reaction shot. Fix: W21 gives only ASKED/SENT (half-scene, "She sent me." "She asked." stops there), keep MENDED for B09 (the file), M22 (the chair) and R41 (costly, mid-confession), and award HELD only to costly protective acts (A28, M26, B22). Rescues cost a field or a Vigour so "rescued from her own composure" has a price.

5. **Birth minute contradicts itself (§186 M03 vs §12 B19).** M03: "You have seen 01:47 on your birth certificate a hundred times"; B19: "You had never known the minute. Now you do." The 147 secret and E_TRUE's closing line depend on the second. Fix M03: "Your certificate has the date and nothing else. Nobody ever told you the minute."

6. **E_TRUE has two small errors in the best section of the book (§200).** (a) "Mum holds up her left hand with her right, the bad hand": her right is "the one that works" two paragraphs up; the bad hand is the left. (b) "in a way you haven't seen since Dad. You haven't, yet. You will, later, in the car." is garbled: she's crying now. Fix: "...with her right, the good one" and "...the way she cried at Dad's funeral and nowhere else. She'll do it again in the car." (or cut the second half).

7. **§165 W11 is false for the tabard reader.** W10 offers "fall in behind her like staff", but W11 says "You hold your ground and let the round come down the ward to you... 'Visitors,' she says fondly." Fix: split W11 into two short paragraphs (badge / tabard), the Sister says "Night staff" to the tabard.

8. **§137 R05 and §38 R39: object continuity.** R05: "VISITOR, St Bede's, and your name in biro", but §58 W16 and §140 W23 explicitly leave the name line blank (only Gary's, §20 W07, gets your name). R39: "You take the ring out of your pocket", but §2 B14 puts it on your little finger and §179 LOCKER has you hold it in your fist. Fix: R05 "...VISITOR, St Bede's, and a line for a name"; R39 "the ring" with no location, or "off your little finger".

9. **§19 M13 and §11 W03: asserts knowledge the reader may not have.** M13: "Bed 3. Your bed. The one by the window on Ward 9." (M10-M13 can be reached without ever opening §87 M18.) W03: "seven hours in a seat and four in a family room": Ros reached the room about ten minutes before the surgeon. Fix: M13 "Bed 3, she writes. You don't know why, yet" (or gate on WARD9); W03 "and a room that wouldn't stop humming".

10. **Cheap-to-find LPA gap (§179 LOCKER, §25 R25).** The LOCKER route gives the letter ("I couldn't make you be the one") but never the LPA form; R25 then has Ros say "I'm the one she named. On the form." as if known. Fix: LOCKER, Ros-present: one line from her ("Page eleven. I signed it too."), and a one-clause echo in R25 for readers without HELD-from-B07.

11. **Tabard doubled (§53 B23, §177 W25, §49 R32).** "You lift it off the hook... It fits you better than your own coat" is near-verbatim in both; B23 says the old Fairy Snow "she gave up" (so it can't be what "she uses"), R32 says it smells of "her washing powder"; name tape is "over the pocket" (B11) vs "inside the neck" (W25). Fix: B23 keeps the Fairy Snow/1979 smell; W25 gets hand cream only and a different fit line; R32 "smells of her".

12. **Staircase arithmetic (§32 B30).** "It's one floor. It should be one floor" but the lift panel is B, G, 1 (§130 HL, §42 HLD, §194 R01 all pass G). Fix: "two floors... four flights".

13. **Ros's tell-tale paragraphs (whole book).** The conditional "*If Ros is with you,*" caption appears in about 75 sections; the commonest verbs are "doesn't say anything" (48 "She doesn't…"), hand "stayed there / flat on the clasp" (§142 B07, §122 H13, §102 M09, §115 M14, §183 W13, §52 R10), "goes still", "looks at X for a long moment". She reads as a nervous witness. Fix: rule for writers: one Ros line per section, and it must change something (a number, a joke, an action); cut at least half the pure-reaction ones; no repeat of the hand-in-bag gesture within a zone.

14. **Ros's "One. Two. Three." list tic overused (§138 B10, §174 B09, §175 M26, §51 H07, §11 W03, §91 R11, §47 R41).** It is a great signature when it's rare (R11 "'One,' she says." after the letter is the one to keep). Fix: keep M26 and R11; vary or cut the rest, and never let her do the list in a rescue scene (B22).

15. **Captioned subtext (§120 A10, §9 R26, §185 R27, §52 R10).** "And you realise it's the most honest thing she has said to you in eight years" (A10), "It was always going to cost something. You didn't know it would cost this." (R26), "you're ashamed of how much easier it is" (R27), "Fields don't come off because you want them off…" (R10). The scenes already show it. Fix: cut each to its image: A10 ends at "'Okay,' she says, and lets go of the clasp."; R27 ends at "it's so much easier"; R10's rule goes into the Notice or a Ros line.

16. **Cold route never meets Mum's flaw (§102 M09, §196 R35).** Without SPOKE/MENDED the player sees Mum only as warm and funny until E_TRUE tells them she steered both children. M09 even says "I sent her, you know" and moves on. Fix: M09, for everyone, add one Mum line to you: "And I kept you, which I'd no business doing" (no Ros detail, no verbatim). That seeds E_TRUE for readers who found nothing.

17. **The Okafors (§143 B07/§153 B08 "D. Okafor", §89 W24 "J. OKAFOR", §126 R20 "Joseph Okafor").** Three unrelated people, three name-checks. If it is a deliberate echo (the hospital reuses names), nothing says so; if not, it reads as a dropped name. Fix: rename the LPA witness (D. Mensah) and leave the 1979 nurse and the Bay 5 patient as the only echo, or make that echo explicit (the nurse's name on Mr Okafor's wristband).

18. **Time of the A&E band (§196 R35).** "the band they put on her in A&E at nine o'clock last night"; §111 A04 has her found at 18:10, ambulance 18:52, CT at 20:15. Fix: "at seven o'clock last night".

19. **"Turn back" that goes forward (§45 R23, §110 R24; §50 R19 → §126 R20).** In the unlucky ward, "turn back" from Bay 3 lands on "Bay 4." and R19→R20 (no MENDED) has no "the ward has turned" bridge, though R26/R27 supply one. Fix: R23's choice text "if something is wrong, you step away from the bed"; add R26's bridge sentence to R19's no-MENDED branch.

20. **Pay-off for Ros's empty draft (§152 H03, §200 E_TRUE).** "THINGS TO SAY TO BEAN" with nothing under it is the best planted object in the book, and the only pay-off is the last line "Get in, Bean", which readers may not connect. Fix: in the car-park beat, Ros's phone lies on the roof, notes app open, title now followed by one line you can't see; she picks it up before you can look. Adds ten words, no new ending beat.
