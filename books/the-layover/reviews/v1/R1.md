# R1: Developmental Edit (Story, Structure, Pacing)
**THE LAYOVER**, reviewed against Playthrough 1 (kind reader, true ending) and Playthrough 4 (reckless reader, Ground Crew ending)

## 1. Verdict: 6/10

Act One is a 9. It is tight, frightening and emotionally exact, and the line-level prose would hold up in a straight horror novel. After the Atrium the book turns into a set of fetch quests linked by long boilerplate hub sections. The scariest beat repeats word for word, the creature stops escalating, and both finales stop for an audit when they should be building.

---

## 2. Top 7 problems (worst first)

**1. The hub loop reads the same 600–800 words again on every return, and it wrecks the book's best scare.**
*Where:* 72 → 29 → 156 → 115. In P1, 115 is read **five times**, 72 four times, and 29 ("*Leave Sam with us*") with its refusal at 156 **three times, word for word**. P4 repeats 115, 175 and 2 three or four times each. About 4,000 words of P1 are text the reader has already read.
*Why it matters:* 29 is your midpoint, the moment the Manifest speaks in Dad's voice. By the third reading it's furniture. 72 claims the creature is "nearer every time you come back", but the text is identical every time, so the book is telling the reader the threat is growing while showing it standing still.
*Fix:* 29 fires **once**, gated by a codeword. Write 72 in three states keyed to how many CLEARED_ codewords the reader holds (1, 2 or 3), each with a new creature position, a new detail and a new clock time. On return visits, cut 115, 175 and 2 to about 60 words of navigation.

**2. The finale stops for paperwork.**
*Where:* 73 → 157 → 64 → 185 → 3 → 173 → 98 → 91. "*Refreshments… Airside. After midnight.*" / "*Accommodation*" / "*Correspondence. Pages answered.*" For the kind reader that's eight sections between 05:00 and 05:41 in which **nothing happens**. For the reckless reader it's a run of bills: throwing up the meal (107), flashing the uniform (55), a dice fight (116).
*Why it matters:* the last act should be compressing time and closing in on the reader. Instead it's an itemised receipt. The kind reader's reward is boredom.
*Fix:* fold the ledger into a single section that branches on debts held, then go straight to the jet bridge. Better still, make the ledger's last and biggest entry the **real** debt: "*Correspondence: one voicemail, 05:59, unplayed.*" Settling it should mean a goodbye or a confession, which puts the emotional spine into the mechanics at the climax.

**3. The Manifest stops escalating after 36.**
*Where:* after the flicker scene (15/36) the tall passenger only stands in the Atrium (72) or appears as a red dot on a screen (172, "*always one room behind you*"). It never acts on that rule. The Claim track has no visible effect: P4 gives its name twice (140, 97), yet at 73 the sign reads "*Your name, most of it*" on both routes, whatever the Claim. The flicker rule is used once and dropped.
*Why it matters:* the horror flattens across the middle half. Side monsters supply the spikes, and the main threat turns into scenery.
*Fix:* stage at least two mid-book encounters that cash in "one room behind": a door you just came through opening, its breathing in the room you just left. Make the sign's letter count literal at 72 and 73, keyed to Claim. Bring the flicker rule back twice, e.g. in the hotel corridor (33) or the lift (151).

**4. The core rule contradicts itself: is boarding LV 137 salvation or death?**
*Where:* 65: "*FLIGHT LV 137 WAS FOUND AT ITS GATE… NONE OF ITS 137 PASSENGERS HAVE BEEN LOCATED*" (they vanished on board). 79, Walter: "*They boarded us, then they brought us off again*" (they merged at the gate). 29: the monster's offer is "*you may board now*". The true ending is boarding.
*Why it matters:* the reader can't tell what victory looks like, so the final choice at 66 ("*walk straight past it into the aircraft*") has no clear stakes.
*Fix:* draw one clear line. The Manifest wants you in **Seat 1A under Dad's booking** (to fill its debt). Escape means boarding on **your own stamped names** (0A/0B). The Hostess says it plainly at 64, and 65 is brought into line with Walter.

**5. The lore is over-explained, and the big reveal gets spent too early.**
*Where:* Walter at 79 says it outright: "*that tall fellow out there in the coat. That's us.*" Then 99 (the manifest list), 65 (the news) and finally 66 ("*there are a hundred and thirty-seven*"). Your own bible says the creature isn't named plainly before §66.
*Why it matters:* the doorway reveal at 66 is the best image in the book, and by then the reader has had it three times.
*Fix:* Walter hints ("*we sat closer… held on*") and never names it. Keep the 137 count and the faces in the coat for 66 alone. Let either 99 or 65 carry the manifest fact, not both.

**6. Sam's relationship state isn't tracked in shared sections, so his arc goes flat.**
*Where:* P4, 141: "*You never let me grieve*", and Sam follows "*a careful ten paces behind*". Two hubs later, in 72, "*He doesn't move away from you. You notice that you're glad*", and in 156 "*close enough that your sleeves touch*". At 184, "*Sam's hand finds yours*" appears on both routes. The 40 snub ("*You'd know. You've been here the whole time*") is never addressed again.
*Why it matters:* a cold route and a warm route converge into the same Sam, so the choices feel like they didn't matter.
*Fix:* add TRUCE/no-TRUCE paragraph variants to 72, 156, 184 and 2. Give the no-TRUCE route one repair scene, or let it reach Gate 0 still fractured. Cut Sam's sleeve-grabbing (10–12 times per route) to three moments that mean something.

**7. The Ground Crew ending turns on a costume choice, not on how the reader played.**
*Where:* the UNIFORM is half-forced at 110 (you take it, then get pushed into the cart regardless). Sam says "*take it off*" at 110, 55 and 108. At 108 nobody has a reason to keep it on. Every reckless act in P4 (FED, CHECKED_IN, MARKED, FORGOT, giving away Dad's voice at 158) was cleared at the ledger, and the ending ignores all of it.
*Why it matters:* the ending is beautifully written, but it's a wrong-door punishment rather than the result of a pattern.
*Fix:* make Ground Crew a threshold ending. It triggers when the reader reaches Gate 0 with FORGOT or Claim 3+ **and** the uniform. Have the Hostess cite those choices: "*You gave it your name twice. You gave it his voice.*" Tie it to the theme in one line: Kite, who always left, is finally made to stay. Remove Sam's third warning so the choice at 108 feels tempting rather than stupid.

## 3. Cut list

- **115 / 72 / 175 / 2 repeat text**: compress to short navigation on return visits (see #1).
- **29 + 156**: fire once only.
- **3, 173, 98**: merge into one ledger section with branches.
- **Jet bridge 13 → 38 → 150 → 56 → 66**: this repeats the travelator three-walk puzzle (129 → 146 → 160 → 176). Keep one. I'd cut the travelator version and keep the bridge, where the coat on the hook earns its place.
- **8 (currency exchange)**: a stat shop with no story. Cut.
- **31 + 65 (TV news)**: redundant with 99. Keep only the newsreader's turning head, or cut.
- **177 (TRANSFER on the cigarette packet)**: duplicates 117's "*Transfer passengers: turn to your flight number.*" Keep one clue.
- **123 stairwell** (dumps you back in the laundry) and **24** (connective filler): cut or fold in.
- **27 vs 76**: the confession is played in full twice on the true route. Make 27 partial (the gate, the fear) and save the voicemail and the goodbye for 76.
- **The "perfect smile, terrified eyes" formula**: used on eight characters. Keep it for three and give the rest a different wrongness.

## 4. Pacing map

- **Act One (1 → 131, about 25 sections): high and well built.** It escalates cleanly from the receipt to the board of Sam's cities. 131 is the real act break, but right now it hands straight to a menu.
- **Act Two (hub quests): low and flat.** Tension resets to zero at every Atrium return. The hotel in P4 runs about 15 sections with three lobby repeats. **Change:** a visible countdown, creature-state escalation, and the midpoint (29) played once at roughly 50%, with something lost when it lands (Sam taken, or the clock jumping an hour).
- **Act Three (73 → end): starts low and finishes high.** 73–98 is dead air. 13–66 builds well. 66/76 is the true climax and it works. **Change:** cut 05:00–05:41 by about 70% and spend those words on 66.
- **Endings:** 200 lands hard. 89 is a strong final image attached to a weak trigger.

## 5. Add list

1. **Pay off Dad's 1998 turn-back.** The flash at 119 ("*You, crying*") is never resolved, and at 27 you say "*I got as far as the gate… and turned round*". That mirror is the book's thesis, and nobody notices it. Give it one beat.
2. **Resolve Dad's 05:59 voicemail** (12: "*I should have told you both why I never…*"), which clashes with Sam's at 200. Pay it off or cut it.
3. **A real low point before Act Three** on the kind route too, not only on SAM_TAKEN routes.
4. **One mid-book scene where the Manifest is in the room with you**, outside a flicker.

## 6. What works

- Act One is tight and frightening, as good as any opening in the genre.
- The board of Sam's invented cities (131) is a superb proof that the airport knows the family.
- The tell (Dad never used your full name) is a rule the reader can actually use, and 29 and 66 pay it off.
- 66/76 (the faces in the coat; a goodbye as the weapon) is a perfect climax, and the prayer room (53) sets it up properly.
- Ending 200 (the voicemail, the window, "*You made it true*") earns its tears. Walter, Ruth and the phone call (189/198) are a lovely subplot payoff.
