# R5: Characters and Emotional Arc

## 1. Verdict: 6/10

Line by line it often knows grief (the downstairs-toilet wall of boarding passes; "he got delayed. They nod."), but the branching flattens the relationship into a few early codewords and shared hub text that is warm no matter what, so Sam often reacts to the plot rather than to you. The prose also keeps explaining feelings it has already earned.

## 2. Top 7 problems (worst first)

**1. Shared sections are warm whatever you have done, so the cold routes go soft.** In 2_cold you snap "calm down" (40) and let Sam answer the Announcer (94). Then the shared text has him take your sleeve "and doesn't let go" (131), with "He doesn't move away from you... you notice that you're glad" (72). He sits "closer than he needs to" (183) and holds your sleeve "and he doesn't let go" (126). The coldness you chose is gone within ten sections. Choices stop mattering; Sam warms up on a timer. *Fix:* make every intimacy line in a hub depend on TRUCE. Write a no-TRUCE version for 72, 126, 131, 145, 183 and 184, where Sam walks a pace ahead, says things to the board rather than to you, and keeps his hands in his pockets.

**2. The hubs make the relationship reset.** In 3b the reader hits 72→29→156 three times and gives the identical "We'll wait together. Whatever it is. We wait together" speech three times. They also read "you notice that you're glad" four times. 115 promises "He'll find a new one every time... a little thinner", then gives "Good for Hollowmouth" word for word every time. *Fix:* give 156 a codeword so it plays once and route repeats to a short line. Write graded versions of the 115 joke: "Hollowmouth's on time." / "Hollowmouth." / silence.

**3. The 3b ending blames the wrong sibling.** In 3b you rescue Sam (20), keep Walter's promise, and confess honestly (76). Because you have no TRUCE, Sam walks out (43), and then 34 tells you "You're good at leaving. You always were" and plays a "Don't leave me..." voicemail. Sam left you. Honesty is punished with a verdict for a different crime. *Fix:* route 43 to its own ending, 34b, "Separate Seats". You both land on the same flight and sit rows apart, and at the funeral he stands on the far side of the coffin. The final image is that the voicemail stays unplayed because *he* won't sit next to you to hear it.

**4. Sam keeps being turned into a child.** "a boy's voice" (88), "thirty and he's two" (88), "about nine years old" (103), "about twelve" (79), "like a little boy in a supermarket" (50), and the bottom bunk (47). This is the man who did fourteen months of tablets and night shifts. It flatters the guilty sibling and erases what Sam did. The best line about him is in 172: "the voice he must have used on the phone to doctors, and you've never heard it before." That is the real Sam, and he gets one sentence. *Fix:* keep at most two age images in the whole book. In the hospital room (181) and the Duty-Free ward smell (135), let Sam take charge, because he knows the room and you don't.

**5. Dad is a collection of lovable props.** Singing badly, peppermints, the darts lads, the coat button done up wrong, "Every gate's a door, Kite." He has no flaw, no grievance and no edge. *Fix:* give him one real fault that bears on the estrangement. For example: he hid the diagnosis from you for months "so you wouldn't worry", or never once asked about your life abroad. Let Sam say something unkind about him, and laugh.

**6. The confession is told twice, and the second time gives away the voicemail.** 27 and 76 repeat each other: "scared of watching you get smaller... let Sam do it. All of it. On his own." In 27 Sam already says "He asked for you... I told him you were coming", which takes the surprise out of the voicemail in 200. *Fix:* if TOLD is set, cut 76 down to "I didn't come. I'm sorry. Goodbye." Cut "I told him you were coming" from 27 so that 200 is the first time we hear it.

**7. The crew are all the same note.** The Hostess, gate clerk, Chef and Night Manager each get "perfect smile / terrified eyes" plus a fresh simile ("car on the wrong side of the road", "heard something downstairs", "trapped under ice"). Only "Don't eat the sandwich" and "They make me" (64) break the note. *Fix:* give each one a human leak from 1998. The Hostess hums a 1998 song as she types; the Night Manager touches a wedding ring on a chain whenever he says "rest".

## 3. Sam

**Keep:** the deflecting humour ("I want you to know in advance it won't be a cool scream"), "I'm not done being angry" (27), the coat he wouldn't put in the hold (88), the playlist, the window seat, and "You have to make it true, or I'm a liar" (200).

**Change:** he has no wants beyond the window seat and not being alone, and no contradictions at all. We know nothing of his life: job, partner, what he gave up. He also has no share of the blame. His "*We're fine*" texts (199) told you not to come, and nobody ever looks at that. Give him bite aimed at *you*. At the moment the narrator delivers his jabs for him: "He doesn't add *not that you'd know*. He doesn't need to" (135).

**Rewrites:**
- 83: "Brilliant. Great. Love that for me." → "*Do not sleep airside.* I haven't slept since March. Let them try."
- 102: "I'm so tired. I did all of it. Every night. The tablets and the sheets and the phone calls and the... I'm so tired." → "You know how you change a bed with someone in it? You roll them. Like a carpet. He used to say *mind the merchandise*." (Pause.) "I got quite good."
- 76: "Bye, Dad. We're all right. We've got each other." → "Bye, Dad." (A beat.) "Your coat's done up wrong." (This calls back to the button in 66, and it is the true kind of goodbye.)

## 4. Sentimentality cuts

- **134:** cut "For a moment you are both kids again... there's only each other." The arm-hold has already done it.
- **102:** cut "It's the most natural thing in the world."
- **86 / 156:** cut "It's what you didn't say, all the times it mattered" and "because it's true and you need to hear it in the air."
- **Kite tell (29, 50, 90, 66):** the "he never called you that" explanation appears four times. Keep it fully once, at 29. After that, just let the full name sit there.
- **76:** cut the italic "*It can't come where one was said.*" It turns a goodbye into a spell.
- **167:** cut "It's Dad's playlist. Sam has been listening to it all night, on repeat, since the bag drop" (the earbud and "Didn't want to be on my own with it" carry it). Also cut Dad's face looking "almost grateful".
- **189 + 198:** Walter is paid off twice. Cut the phone call or cut it to three lines. In 198 cut "the lightest you've ever seen anyone walk" and the sign folded "in half again".
- **200:** cut "I meant it. The liar thing. You made it true" and keep "Took you long enough." Cut "He means it.", "You hold him up." and "For the first time in three years, you don't go anywhere. You stay." End on "Safe landings, Kite."
- **34:** cut the final line, "You're good at leaving. You always were." "You don't play that one either" is the ending.

## 5. Missing beats

1. **What started it three years ago.** What was said when you moved? "Three years" is currently just a number.
2. **Sam's share.** A moment where he admits the "We're fine" texts were meant to keep you out: pride, control, the wish to be the one Dad needed. This turns penitent-and-saint into two siblings.
3. **The gate parallel.** In 27 you "got as far as the gate... and turned round". In 1998 Dad turned back at the gate *for* you. The book's best idea, unnoticed. Let Sam say it.
4. **The coat fight.** Sam brought the coat and didn't tell you (88). One sharp exchange about who gets to carry it, and it pays off when it hangs on the hook in 150.
5. **Sam leads you.** One scene, ideally 181, where you break and Sam handles it in the doctor-phone voice, and you see what fourteen months made him.

## 6. What works

- The Alone ending (34) is the best writing in the book: the empty seat, "look", the dipping cushion.
- "You have to make it true, or I'm a liar": specific, guilty, and it hurts.
- In route 2, Sam's "Kite, please" at 92 is the only time that name from him lands with full force.
- In the window-seat payoff (200), the decision to tell it in retrospect ("you will only ever tell them one thing") avoids milking it.
- Walter: peppermints, "joining up", the hand grown into the armrest.
