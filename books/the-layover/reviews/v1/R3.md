# R3 — Prose line edit: THE LAYOVER

Read in full: playthroughs 1 (90 sections) and 2 (60 sections). That is 102 unique sections, about 26k words. Counts are per unique section. Hubs 72, 115, 29 and 156 are read 4–5 times in playthrough 1, so the reader meets everything more often than the numbers show.

## 1. Verdict — 6.5/10

The voice is controlled, concrete and often frightening, and Sam is a real person on the page. But the book leans on a dozen moves and narrates its own feelings so often that by the Atrium the reader can see the machinery working before the dread arrives.

## 2. Top 7 language problems

**1. Smiling mouth, frightened eyes: seven uses, and then the book explains it.** "Her smile is perfect… Her eyes are terrified." (153). "Every tooth exactly where it should be. Above it, his eyes are screaming." (42). It recurs in 82, 62, 50 ("They are frightened. They are so frightened."), 66 and 73. Then 121 spells it out: "The eyes are us… trying to tell you." The image weakens with each repeat. By the Chef it reads like a stat block. **Rule:** keep three: the Hostess, Dad at the window, Dad in the doorway. The other staff each need their own tell (the Night Manager's missing reflection already works). Cut the explanation in 121.

**2. Simile density.** There are 87 "the way…", 58 "as if/as though" and 66 "like a…". That is about one comparison every 125 words, and often two per sentence. Example, 15: "buzzing like flies trapped in a jar, the concourse jumping in and out of existence like a film with frames missing." Sam gets an age tag in 12 places: "he looks about nine years old" (103), "He sounds about twelve" (79), "he's thirty and he's two and every age between" (88). "Used to" appears 21 times. **Rule:** one simile per section. One childhood flashback per Sam scene, and none in hubs.

**3. Captioning the subtext.** "He doesn't ask whether you did. That's worse." (165). "Kept a promise, maybe. Or made one." (93). "It's awkward. It's the most natural thing in the world." (102). "That feels like something." (145). The gesture lands, and then the narrator explains it. That is the opposite of the Jackson restraint the bible asks for. **Rule:** if the line after a gesture names what it meant, delete it.

**4. "Not X. Y."** 39 sentences open with "Not". "Not both. One." (21). "Not shivering. Shaking." (166). "Not unzipped. Opened." (88). "Not two seats away. Not one. Beside you." (145). Add 73 paragraphs of four words or fewer outside the choice lines. **Rule:** one corrective negation per section. Say the true thing first.

**5. Superlatives and intensifiers.** "the best smile you have ever seen on a human face" (153). "the hardest thing you've ever asked your body to do" (36). "the loudest thing you've ever heard", said of a silence (27). "the hardest thing you've done all night, and you've done some hard things" (169). "a sound… you will keep for the rest of your life" (189). Then there are 51 "very"s, and 8 people who go "very still". **Rule:** no "ever" superlatives in narration. Leave them to Sam ("the most stupid sunglasses in the world" is his register).

**6. Hubs and phone rhythm.** Section 115 is read five times word for word. Its narrator lines go stale: "He'll find a new one every time… a little thinner." / "It doesn't wait for anyone." Section 72 has "You notice that. You notice that you're glad." Eighteen paragraphs run past 75 words, for example 143 (106), 13 (108) and 126 (109), against the bible's 1–4 sentence rule. There are 39 SAM_TAKEN codas inside scenes where Sam is present: "Sam stands at your shoulder" is followed by "there is no one at your shoulder." **Rule:** cut hubs to three plain paragraphs with no emotional narration. Cap paragraphs at 60 words. Move SAM_TAKEN text into separate variants.

**7. Dialogue.** Sam's comic voice is the best thing in the book. His grief beats repeat, though. "He asked for you… I told him you were busy" (119) becomes "He asked for you… I told him you were coming" (27), which contradicts it. Your confession runs at length twice in one route (27, 76). Dad "sang badly" four times. At the climax Sam goes greeting-card: "We're all right. We've got each other." (76). Your own speech is too articulate for someone falling apart: "Scared of watching you get smaller. Scared of the room, and the smell…" Clarity slips:
- In 72 Sam is against you, and in 29, which follows directly, he is "three steps away".
- 13's "walk it clean three times" assumes the reader was taught this rule. On this route they weren't.
- "Love that for me" (83) will date fast. **Rule:** each fact gets one line, once. If 27 has fired, 76 must be short and new.

## 4. Ten line edits

1. **153** "Her smile is perfect. It is the best smile you have ever seen on a human face, every tooth in its place…" → "She is smiling. Every tooth in its place."
2. **36** "It's the hardest thing you've ever asked your body to do. Every animal part of you wants to see." → "Every animal part of you wants to see."
3. **27** "the silence of the white beyond the window is the loudest thing you've ever heard." → "Beyond the window, the white makes no sound at all."
4. **102** "It's awkward. It's the most natural thing in the world." → "Your elbow's in the wrong place. You leave it there."
5. **72** "He doesn't move away from you. You notice that. You notice that you're glad." → "He stays where he is, against your arm."
6. **15** "buzzing like flies trapped in a jar, the concourse jumping in and out of existence like a film with frames missing." → "Off, on, off. The tubes buzz like flies in a jar."
7. **76** "We're all right. We've got each other." → "It's all right. We've got it." (This echoes his armour line from 21, "I've got it", made plural.)
8. **94** "and you count. Sixty. A hundred. A hundred and thirty-seven." → "and you count, and lose count, and start again."
9. **189** "a sound comes down the line that you will keep for the rest of your life: an old man laughing, or crying, in a way that sounds like something heavy being set down at last." → "an old man laughing, or crying. You can't tell which. The line clicks."
10. **26** "empty and ordinary and a little sad, the way every airport is at that hour, and you can't believe how badly you want to cry about it." → "It's just a terminal, empty the way they all are at that hour. Your eyes sting anyway."
