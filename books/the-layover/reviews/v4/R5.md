## 3. Top 5 problems now

**1. The Sam blocks have no memory, so the middle of the book thins him.** Sam becomes a commentator on rooms rather than a brother reacting to you. The only state any block checks is TRUCE, so cold Sam is written as the absence of warm Sam: "keeps to the far side… *If TRUCE,* crosses over instead." After 176 his hand carries an ink letter he spat on and scrubbed, and not one block between 176 and 86 mentions it. After 8, the next blocks carry on as though that conversation never happened. *Fix:* add MARKED clauses in three hub blocks (22: he rubs the back of his hand on his jeans; 19: he presses the hollow 0 with the *other* thumb; 76: he tries the handles left-handed). Give cold Sam one beat in 113 that isn't a subtraction from the TRUCE one.

**2. CARE still ignores the hardest act of care.** At 16 you are dragged across the carpet holding your brother back from Gate 00, and you pass. You get nothing for it, so 69's "I know what you did tonight" is withheld from exactly the reader who did the most. *Fix:* success at 16 gives CARE if you don't already have it.

**3. The voicemail's truth contradicts the family memories.** Dad "never flew again after '98", when Kite was about six and Sam about three. Yet Sam remembers "Four hours early. Every holiday" (22), and Dad "wore it to every airport" (179). *Fix:* 22 becomes "Four hours early. Even just to watch." 179 becomes "He wore it to the viewing deck every Sunday."

**4. FORGOT costs nothing in Separate Seats.** At 29 the Hostess takes the sound of "Kite", and the jet bridge says it back "exactly right". Arrival pays it off ("a stranger's voice, until it says *Kite*"). But in Separate Seats, Sam's "Not today" doesn't know it is withholding the only recording that could give the sound back. *Fix:* in 114, *If you have FORGOT*: "It's the only place he still says it right." Then "Not today."

**5. Arrival still over-ends.** "Or I'm a liar" is the line. "I can't be the only one left" just explains it. *Fix:* cut that sentence and the shoulder lean. Put "Safe landings, Kite" on the tape as Dad's sign-off, before "Has Kite landed?" End on "Took you long enough."

## 4. Characters

**Sam.** His best material is new or survived the move into blocks: "Why was my passport on the pillow like a mint", "That's the ward… You'd have got used to it" (a barb disguised as a tip), and "That's not even how you spell it." 95's "Nothing. We'll wait. Together." reads as Sam's line; give it its own paragraph. The 19 block (thumb in the hollow 0) has no revisit skip, so route 6 prints it three times.

**The reader.** The arc is earned. THAW is a hand on an arm, CARE is a hand over a mouth, and TRUCE costs a sentence about knowing what "fine" meant. A cold reader can still reach TRUCE at 115, which is right. Check whether 25's "Cross off SIGNED" lets 133 fire twice. If it does, Sam crosses you out again and the gesture cheapens.

**Kite.** The FORGOT economy (the Chef or the Hostess takes the sound, and the tape returns it) is the best idea added in this revision. Sam's own mouth still never says the name.

**Dad.** The voicemail is now a real person. He turned round for a crying child and the flight went without him. "Don't you two turn round" pulls against Rule 7. Leave that tension unresolved.

**Endings vs routes.** Alone is excellent: "You sit by the window, which you never get" answers 200's "You never get it." Separate Seats is right in spirit, but see #4 and the unconfessed lie.
