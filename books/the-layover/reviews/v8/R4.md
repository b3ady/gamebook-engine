## 3. Top 5 problems now

1. **Claim 3 is still a free refund.** The Claim 4 bar only helps if a run can actually reach 4, and both of my runs topped out at 3. P5 died twice and still got wiped to 1. Cross off 1 letter, not 2. Or keep a scar: let the stamp at 45 smudge your name *for good*, and make that change the 120 ending.
2. **Gate 0 is ten sections of admin.** "One-page reckoning" describes 30, but 89, 165, 45 and 82 are all desk pages too. Fold 89 into 172, and 165 and 45 into 30. Keep 82. Get me to the bridge by page four.
3. **61 is a wall.** It has five If-blocks, all printed for every reader, every visit. That's about 300 words, six times over. Keep the passenger meter; shrink the "first time" block to a skip line, the way 70 and 77 do it.
4. **The secret is clued twice over.** Cut 13's packet or 49's tag, and the 120 nudge. Let me feel clever.
5. **It still explains its own tells, and reuses its own lines.** Cut "It doesn't call you Kite" and "It doesn't know that." *"Many mouths, none of them together"* appears verbatim at 16 and 44. The breathing choir is at 116, 186, 64, 16 and 44. Uneven shoulders are at 2, 197, 16 and 120.

## 4. Scariest moments, ranked

1. **20, nothing wrong.** *"There must be something, because there has always been something."* Still the best page in the book.
2. **177/63, the hospital bed.** The chest and the monitor are out of time with each other, there's *"Stay with your old dad, lad,"* and then a shoulder at the foot of the bed.
3. **83, answering the Announcer.** Your name comes out of your own mouth in his voice, and maintenance closes the ticket: *NO FAULT FOUND. The name goes on.* That's pure analog horror, the best death in the book.
4. **34, the silent aircraft.** You can hear the joins tick.
5. **189, "the hum you've heard all night was never an aircraft."** A great retroactive chill, still undercut by the Kite line.
6. **197, the lift.** It goes down to 0, waits, and comes back up, and the sign shows one letter per point of Claim.
7. **2, the sign.** A child's hand with a plaster, and the first letter of your name in *wet* marker.
8. **81, the loop.** *"This time, you can read it,"* and Sam's name has been started.

## 5. Lore

**What hooks me:**
- **1952.** Room 1952 is Dad's birth year and the year the old terminal opened. It's chilling and quiet.
- **The manifest.** 137 names ruled through, and seat 1A says *DID NOT BOARD*. With *"Turned round at a gate once"* and your March turn-around, that's the spine of the book.
- **Walter and Ruth.** *"She always made a sign"*, and the thing holds an arrivals sign.
- **Sam's cities** on the board, then on the hotel clocks, then *PORT EVER. DEPARTED.* *"I made that one up." "I know. He kept it."*
- **The stockroom** of named luggage boxes, and the rota crossed off to 23/11/1998.

**What falls flat:**
- **1987.** The receipt now plants it, so it's no longer random. But "somebody else's family" is sequel bait, not lore.
- **4's** *"...not hers... should have kept..."* still goes nowhere.
- **The Night Manager's ring.** Whose? A hook with no line.

## 8. What works

- **The bridge (11/168/34/20/35).** Peak Exit 8.
- **The Claim meter in the world.** It shows on the sign at 197, 16 and 121, and through the stamp at 45. You *see* your score.
- **The 16 clock.** "Something has been keeping your night for you."
- **163 → 156 → 137.** Still the peak, and 137 lands harder without the guard.
- **Sam.** *"I'm not done being angry,"* *"Why was my passport on the pillow like a mint,"* *"It talks in crossword clues,"* *"Your coat's done up wrong."*
- **83's death.** It's the only rewind I didn't resent.
