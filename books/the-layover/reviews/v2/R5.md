## 3. Top 5 problems now

**1. Cold Sam is a single gesture, and only words change him.** "A pace ahead, hands in his pockets" appears 9 times in route 6 and 6 times in route 2. Only two dialogue choices grant TRUCE (120, 51), plus the LOST rescue. In route 6 you drag him back from Gate 00 while he says "Please don't let go" (50), fetch his passport, bleed for the page, and refuse Dad's voice with "We'll wait. Together." None of it registers. v1 inverted: then he warmed on a timer, now an early choice freezes him. *Fix:* add a THAW tally for acts of care (74, 50, 96, 140). At THAW 2 or more, no-TRUCE Sam walks beside you without touching, and his text at 163 changes. He still leaves, but he says "I know what you did tonight. It doesn't cover March."

**2. The reader's side goes unanswered at the climax.** Sam's 167 admission is the best scene in the book. Choosing "he never asked you to" then cuts straight to the hub with no reply at all. At 163, Sam's charge sheet ("You let me do it alone") forgets that he said "Don't bother coming back" on this same route. *Fix:* give the cold choice its own short section. Sam: "No. I didn't." A beat. "Would you have come?" You don't answer. Then at 163 give him one flinch: "I told you not to. I know. You still didn't."

**3. The voicemails collide.** Route 6 opens on "*Dad. Received 05:59.*" and ends on "*Sam. 05:59.* The voicemail from the night Dad died", which that route never introduced. Dad's "I should have told you both why I never…" is never paid off on any route. The Hostess's "1 MSG · 05:59 · UNHEARD" could be either message. *Fix:* in 111, show two notifications, Sam's unplayed and Dad's impossible one. Pay off Dad's in Arrival or Connection (why he never flew again), or cut it.

**4. The KID variant reads like a placeholder.** "Night, Kite. Night, my kid." "Come on, my kid." "May I offer you a complimentary refreshment voucher, passenger?" *Fix:* use "Night, Kite. Night, kiddo" or "Night, love" (Dad already says "love" at 169). For the Hostess, drop the honorific in the KID variant: "May I offer you a complimentary refreshment voucher?" SON and DAUGHTER are fine.

**5. Sam is an adult in what he can do, but he has no life of his own.** He has no job, partner or flat, and nothing he gave up that we can name. *Fix:* give him one line in 181 or 167. "I gave the flat up in May. Didn't see the point paying for a bed I wasn't in."

## 4. Characters

**Sam.** His bite is now aimed at you: "Course. You'd know. You've been here the whole time." His competence is on the page (30, 22, 84). His warmth is earned on the warm route; on cold routes it is withheld well but without variety. Section 65 ("I've got you") is rightly unconditional: decency isn't warmth.

**The reader.** The March gate confession works. The 11 bluff ("*I'll try to get back next month*") is excellent. The new 67 line, "You kept it from me four months. I stayed away fourteen", is score-keeping in the middle of a goodbye. Either let the thing in the coat react, or cut it.

**Dad (Ray).** He now has a flaw (hiding the diagnosis), an edge (the "flat, end-of-the-day" tone you use at 127) and the '98 turn-back. On cold routes he is still mostly props, because 181 and 33 are warm-only. Put the four-months fact on a shared section.

**Hostess/Margaret.** In 52 she becomes a person: "tired, about fifty, lipstick put on for a shift in 1998." Her name reveal ("Nobody's asked in twenty-eight years") is route 4 only. Seed "Margaret" in 52.

**Walter.** "We never got on" gives him the book's parallel estrangement, and it is the quiet best of the revision. It still pays off twice.

**Crew.** The Chef ("A hundred and thirty-six... One.") and the standby clerk now feel like individuals. The Night Manager touches his ring four times in route 6. Keep two of those.
